# Copyable response recipes

Use these recipes when drafting question reminders or when unsure about a recurring formatting choice. Keep words and meaning specific to the task; reuse the syntax instead of inventing another format.

## Rainbow question reminders

Use rainbow only for the short quoted user-question reminder above ⮑. Preserve the user's meaning and perspective, keeping their wording where possible and naming the resolved subject when a pronoun or vague phrase would be unclear later. Leave the full annotation/evidence block, source viewers, code, links, answer and About footer under their existing rules. These colours identify quoted context; they do not mean success, warning or failure. The quote is the explicit exception to semantic colour meanings and self-contained highlight chunks.

Use exactly one colour per word, generated by the helper from a formula rather than a stored list. Each word has a position on one shared, ever-growing route. Every 6 words the colour reaches a new destination, then blends evenly to the next one. All colours stay on the bright ring of the original 24-colour palette, with one RGB channel at 250 and one at 112. The old colours are exact points on it, such as `#fa7070` red and `#70fa70` green. Blends therefore travel around the colour wheel through the colours in between instead of fading through grey. Short quotes show part of one or two blends; do not stretch a blend to fill the text.

Destination `b` sits at hue tick `(247·b + 414·flip(b) + nudge(b)) mod 828`, where each tick is one distinct shade on the original bright ring. `flip` (0 or 1) and `nudge` (0–70 ticks) come from a fixed integer scrambler (the SplitMix64 finaliser) applied to `b`. New destinations can lie on either side of the current colour; this changes the local route instead of reversing one fixed rainbow. Blends cover about 42–138° in six evenly spaced word steps. Sampled directions are approximately balanced and average about 15° per word, like the old cycle. Every destination is computed directly with integer arithmetic: even word 10³⁰ needs no replay of earlier words. The operation count per word is fixed; arithmetic on exceptionally large integers still grows with their bit length.

The full colour stream repeats after **1242 × 2⁶⁴ = 22,910,856,139,547,263,107,072 word positions**. The stored counter keeps increasing; only the derived colour stream cycles. This is not an enumeration of every possible gradient or colour permutation. With 828 displayable shades there are `828!` orders using each shade once (a 2,059-digit number), but this generator follows a constrained smooth stream. Short excerpts can repeat much sooner than the whole stream, and similar-looking excerpts remain possible. A fixed diagnostic sample of 10,000 consecutive 12-word excerpts contained no exact repeated colour profiles; that is evidence about this sample, not a uniqueness guarantee.

### Why that is the full-stream period

The 64-bit scrambler is bijective. Its derived value `414·flip + nudge` occupies two separate 71-value bands, and has a full `2⁶⁴` input period: at least one output has an odd count across that input cycle, ruling out every smaller power-of-two period. The two bands have unequal residue-count distributions because `2⁶³` is not divisible by 71, so shifting between the bands cannot disguise an earlier destination repeat. A destination period must therefore preserve both the scrambler pattern and the 828-tick drift. Since 247 is coprime with 828, the least such blend period is `lcm(828, 2⁶⁴) = 207·2⁶⁴`.

Each of the 828 destination ticks maps to a different displayed colour. Sampling the word stream every six words therefore recovers the destination stream. Its prime factors force a word period to include the extra factor of six, giving `1242·2⁶⁴`. This proof concerns the entire ongoing stream; it does not prohibit equal short substrings or imply visually unique replies.

Choose a self-contained, relevant question reminder of around 60–80 visible characters when that much relevant wording exists, with a maximum of 80. For an unclear `if it's like a one-time thing` about pausing hidden components, use `Can we pause the whole hidden component at once?`; leave the original quote unchanged in the context block. Do not guess missing context or include the answer. User request — 2026-10-04. Keep short questions short and let longer reminders wrap naturally; do not shorten them merely to fit one line. The helper rejects longer Markdown reminders before moving the colour counter. It produces a short sans-serif `\(\textsf{...}\)` expression with one `\color{#rrggbb}{Word}` for each word. Do not force a long reminder onto one line with a no-wrap layout: that would clip in narrow panes. Do not hand-write these expressions, use per-letter markup or combine words into coloured phrases. Punctuation attached to a word stays with it; each whitespace-delimited word uses one route position.

Keep a single word, identifier or URL longer than 24 visible characters in inline code rather than a wide math box; it still uses its route position, so the blend spacing stays steady. Prefer a genuinely shorter relevant excerpt to colouring a whole message. A helper avoids reconstructing syntax, but the emitted LaTeX still consumes output tokens; keep reminders short.

### Generate without reconstructing the syntax

Save the relevant excerpt in a UTF-8 plain-text file, then run:

```sh
python3 /path/to/response-preferences/scripts/rainbow-quote.py /tmp/question-1.txt /tmp/question-2.txt
```

Omit `--start-index` for normal use. For every final reply, collect its short question excerpts first and pass all their files to **one invocation** in display order. The helper takes the lock once and reads the next route position. It reserves one position per word for every input file, so each quote continues where the previous one ended. It then saves the position after the last word; each printed blockquote is separated by a blank line. The saved position is a plain whole number that only grows; it never wraps. State lives at `$CODEX_HOME/state/response-preferences/rainbow-next-word.txt` (default `~/.codex/state/...`), outside the published skill. A missing file starts at 0. The older `rainbow-next-index.txt` beside it is left untouched: the previous helper and Agent Flow's `AuthoredTextRainbow.swift` still use it for their 0–23 palette index and write it back modulo 24. Sharing it would keep pulling this route back to its start. A damaged file, a negative number, signs, separators or decimals are reported and never overwritten. A lock and atomic replacement protect concurrent tasks. This needs no model decision, network call, repeated process launch or per-quote state write. Concurrent tasks may display replies out of reservation order; a failed generation may consume positions. Reuse the generated quote unchanged on retries. Use `--start-index N` with any whole number from 0 upwards, written in digits, only for reproducible examples/tests. With several files it continues from that pinned position and does not read or advance the counter. `--long-demo` (Markdown or HTML, requires `--start-index`) lifts the 80-character limit for showing a long stretch of the route; normal reminders keep every limit.

Keep the In response to paragraph and the helper's matching rainbow question inside one continuous blockquote. Insert a bare `>` line between their quoted paragraphs, then leave the ⮑ answer outside. An unquoted blank line between the two quoted paragraphs would create two separate left-hand lines. User-requested — 2026-11-09.

The helper handles word colours and TeX escaping, validates the generated expressions with bundled KaTeX, and prints a Markdown blockquote to copy unchanged. No network or new integration is involved. It normalises whitespace to ordinary spaces, preserving the words and punctuation. Append any context link **outside** the colour expressions. Use `--format html` only for website examples; it escapes HTML and uses the same words and colours. The website's examples use one continuous route from position 0 in page order.

The general reply checker excludes quoted evidence, including historical broken syntax; it therefore does **not** validate the new formatting inside a quote. Use the helper, or validate the authored expressions directly with `math-validation.py`'s `render_errors`. This does not alter literal quoted evidence or add another automatic guard.

### Different lengths

These synthetic questions show the same word-by-word rhythm for short and longer reminders. Each is pinned with `--start-index 0`, so all three begin at the same point on the route. In normal use, each quote continues from the previous one. Longer reminders split into expressions of at most 64 visible characters.

```latex
> \(\textsf{\color{#70f9fa}{Why?}}\)
```

```latex
> \(\textsf{\color{#70f9fa}{Can} \color{#70faea}{you} \color{#70fada}{make} \color{#70fac9}{this} \color{#70fab8}{easier} \color{#70faa8}{to} \color{#70fa97}{read?}}\)
```

```latex
> \(\textsf{\color{#70f9fa}{Can} \color{#70faea}{you} \color{#70fada}{keep} \color{#70fac9}{this} \color{#70fab8}{rainbow} \color{#70faa8}{easy} \color{#70fa97}{to} \color{#70faba}{read?}}\)
```

In the last example, the colour blends from sky blue to green over six words, then turns back towards cyan.

## Other recurring choices

| Situation | Reuse this decision |
| --- | --- |
| A saved/merged/deployed result | Green on the short confirmed outcome; ordinary details follow. |
| Useful explanation of how it works | Cyan on a complete short clause; do not turn it green merely because the task is finished. |
| Automated test result | 🧪 and ordinary text, with an explicit passed/failed/unverified status. |
| Needs the user's next action / mainly a takeaway | Favour 🫵 near the end / 👉 with the summary at the top respectively, normally once and only in the final reply. |
| One statement / several blocks | Inline marker / standalone marker plus the existing raised chevron. Keep ⮑ beside its answer in either case. |
| Literal symbols in prose | Keep numbers and symbols visible. Short styled text uses the core symbol lookup: `10\%`, `\$500`, `C\#`, `sample\_tool`. Ordinary Markdown needs no TeX escape. Code, paths and exact quotes retain their own formatting. The quote helper escapes its short literals. |
| Returning after hours | Final-only About: concrete subject, then one useful result or next step in short lavender chunks. |

For working and final replies, use `\(\underline{\textsf{short useful clue}}\)` for a short scanning cue. Keep its inner `\textsf` for the sans-serif font, but do not wrap the whole surrounding sentence or paragraph in another LaTeX expression for grouping. That extra wrapper did not give whole-line triple-click selection and can overflow. Keep normal-size markers in working updates. A selected non-underlined colour uses a separate sans-serif `\(\textsf{\color{#hex}Short fact.}\)` pattern; keep each expression below 64 visible characters and let surrounding Markdown wrap.
